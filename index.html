How Argentix works

Light from your photo exposes a simulated film. The film is developed, then printed on a paper and scanned, as in a lab. Slide film is viewed directly, so it has no paper.

Stop
A doubling or halving of light.
Film exposure
How much light the film received. The printer corrects brightness, as a lab would, so over- and underexposure show as changes in contrast, colour and grain.
Paper
The print material. Each paper has its own contrast and palette. Cinema print films (2383, 2393) give the cinema look.
Print exposure
How long the enlarger exposes the paper: lighter or darker prints.
Yellow and magenta filters
The enlarger's colour filters, in CC units. More yellow makes the print bluer; more magenta makes it greener.
Grain
The visible texture of the film's dye clouds. It scales with your photo's size on a 35 mm frame.
Halation
Light that bounces back through the film base and glows around bright areas.
Tungsten films
Vision3 200T and 500T are made for indoor lamp light.
